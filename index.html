Test whether a reusable takeaway-container service works before claiming it works.

The prototype operationalises the evidence requirements identified in the business plan. Uptake, recovery, return time, completed reuse, condition, washing, loss, logistics and funding are treated as separate variables rather than compressed into a single success rate.

ANALYTICAL EVIDENCE

Observed reuse programmes do not support one "expected" return rate

Cross-case evidence is displayed side-by-side to preserve variation. The business decision is therefore based on separate adoption, recovery and completed-cycle evidence rather than an assumed EcoLoop success percentage.

Reported return rate

Design consequences

Adoption and return are separate. Glasgow reported high return relative to only 3% average uptake, so both denominators are recorded separately.

Dense geography is retained for the first pilot. Network-density evidence supports acquisition benefits, while dispersed collection can weaken recovery and logistics.

No published return rate becomes an EcoLoop target. Sheffield's 98.6% is retained as a case-specific benchmark rather than a forecast.

FUNCTIONAL PILOT PROTOTYPE

Operate the physical loop as a controlled state machine

A completed reuse cycle requires issue, return, washing, inspection and reissue. Lost and retired assets are treated as exception states, and issue/return timestamps support outstanding-stock and return-time analysis.

Data minimisation: do not enter customer names, email addresses, payment details or other personal data in transaction references or working notes.
